# Philippine coffee varieties

The Philippines grows four commercial coffee varieties. Knowing the difference makes it easier to pick a bag.

## Arabica

Grown in the highlands, especially Benguet and the wider Cordillera, and in the mountains of Mindanao. Arabica is smoother and brighter, with floral, fruity or chocolate notes. Try [Caffeine Brothers Benguet Arabica](https://caffeinebrothers.co/product/arabica-coffee-beans/), a medium roast that suits pour-over, drip and espresso.

## Robusta

The bulk of local production, much of it from Mindanao. Robusta is bolder and more bitter, with roughly twice the caffeine of Arabica. It is common in blends and in milk drinks.

## Liberica (Kapeng Barako)

Liberica is the bean behind Kapeng Barako, closely linked with Batangas and Cavite. The beans are large and irregular, and the cup is strong, smoky and woody. See [Kapeng Barako from Caffeine Brothers](https://caffeinebrothers.co/product/kapeng-barako-coffee-beans/), a dark roast of Batangas Liberica.

## Excelsa

Often grouped with Liberica, Excelsa is tart and fruity and is mostly used to add depth to blends.

## Which should you buy?

| If you like... | Try |
|---|---|
| Smooth, bright black coffee | Arabica |
| Strong coffee with milk | Robusta or a blend |
| Bold, smoky, traditional | Kapeng Barako (Liberica) |
